The most common QR code scam is low-tech: someone prints their own code and sticks it over yours, so scans go to a fake payment or login page. You can’t stop someone from carrying stickers, but you can make tampering obvious, check your codes, and help people spot a fake before they pay.

## How QR code tampering works

- **Sticker over a real code.** On parking meters, charging points, restaurant tables, posters and payment counters. The fake code opens a look-alike page that asks for card details or a login.
- **Swapped printed material.** A whole flyer or table card replaced with a copy carrying a different code.
- **A changed destination.** If a dynamic code’s account is taken over, the code itself is untouched but sends scans somewhere else.

## Make your codes tamper-evident

- **Print the code into the material.** A code printed on the sign, menu or window is hard to cover neatly; a separate sticker is easy to swap.
- **Put it behind something.** A clear acrylic stand, a laminate or a frame makes a sticker on top stand out.
- **Show where it goes.** Print the destination in words beside the code, such as “maplecafe.ca/pay”, so people can compare it with what their phone shows.
- **Use a recognisable design.** A code in your colours with your logo and a frame is harder to imitate on the spot than a plain black square.
- **Check them regularly.** Make it part of opening up: look for stickers, peeled edges or anything different, and scan one code a day.

## Protect your dynamic codes

- **Secure the way you sign in.** On QRfinity that’s your email or Google account, so protect it with a strong password and two-step verification, and sign out on shared computers.
- **Watch the dashboard.** Check now and then that each code still goes where it should, and that scan counts look normal.
- **Use your own pages.** Point codes at your own website or your payment provider’s official pages, so people recognise the address.

## Codes that take payments need extra care

Payment codes are the main target, because a fake one pays the scammer directly. For [payment links](/qr-code-generator/payment-link), [UPI](/qr-code-generator/upi) and [Pix](/qr-code-generator/pix):

- Print your business name beside the code, and tell customers to check it matches the name on the payment screen.
- Keep the code where staff can see it.
- After any suspicious report, check every payment code before you open the next day.

## Help people scan safely

The same advice protects your customers and you:

- **Read the address before opening it.** Phones show a preview; if it doesn’t match the business, don’t open it.
- **Be wary of codes that ask for a payment, a login or an app download,** especially on stickers.
- **Type the address yourself** when in doubt.

## Report a bad code

If you find a QRfinity code (a qrfinity.link address) that leads somewhere harmful, [report it](/report) and we’ll review it. For other codes, report the page to the brand it imitates and, if money was lost, to the police and your bank.
