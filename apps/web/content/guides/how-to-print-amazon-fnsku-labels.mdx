To print Amazon FNSKU labels, get each product’s FNSKU from Seller Central, make a label with the barcode, the FNSKU, the product title and the condition, and print it at 100% on a thermal printer or a sheet of labels. Our [FNSKU label generator](/barcode-generator/amazon-fnsku) makes the label at your printer’s exact resolution, so the bars print crisp and Amazon’s warehouse scanners read them first time.

## Step 1: Find your FNSKUs

In Seller Central, open **Inventory**, then **Manage FBA inventory**. The FNSKU is the 10-character code shown for each product, usually starting with X00. If you have many products, download the inventory report: its FNSKU and product name columns paste straight into the [bulk generator](/barcode-generator/bulk).

## Step 2: Pick your printer and label size

| Printer                                                | Label                                                 | Choose in the generator                |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Zebra, Rollo, MUNBYN and other 4-inch thermal printers | 2.25 × 1.25 in rolls                                  | 2.25 × 1.25 in thermal, 203 or 300 dpi |
| DYMO LabelWriter                                       | DYMO 30334 (2.25 × 1.25 in)                           | 2.25 × 1.25 in thermal                 |
| Smaller thermal rolls                                  | 2 × 1 in                                              | 2 × 1 in thermal                       |
| Laser or inkjet printer                                | 30 per US Letter sheet (like Avery 5160) or A4 sheets | The matching sheet size                |

Check your printer’s resolution on its label or spec sheet: most Zebra and Rollo printers are 203 dpi; some are 300 dpi.

## Step 3: Make the labels

1. Open the [FNSKU label generator](/barcode-generator/amazon-fnsku) and enter the FNSKU.
2. Add the product title. Long titles are shortened with an ellipsis in the middle, as Amazon allows.
3. Choose the condition (New, Used – Like New and so on).
4. Pick your label size and printer resolution, and check the scan test passes.
5. Download the PNG (free), or a PDF or ZPL file with a paid plan.

For a whole shipment, use the [bulk generator](/barcode-generator/bulk): one label per row, all in one download.

## Step 4: Print at 100%

This is where most label problems come from.

- **Turn off “fit to page” or “scale”.** Choose actual size or 100%. Scaling changes bar widths and makes some bars thicker than others.
- **Set the paper size to your label size** in the printer dialog, not Letter or A4, when printing to a thermal printer.
- **Zebra printers:** for the sharpest result, send the ZPL file; the printer draws the barcode itself.
- **Label sheets:** print one test page on plain paper first and hold it over a label sheet to check alignment.

## Step 5: Check before you ship

- Scan a printed label with a phone scanner app or a barcode gun: it should read the FNSKU exactly.
- Make sure the label covers any original UPC or EAN on the item.
- Use labels that peel off cleanly; Amazon asks for removable adhesive on most items.

## Labels won’t scan?

- **Faint print:** clean the print head and raise the darkness setting on a thermal printer.
- **Uneven bars:** you printed at a scale other than 100%, or used an image made for another dpi. Download the label for your printer’s resolution.
- **Label cut off:** the printer’s label size doesn’t match the roll. Set it in the printer settings, then calibrate the printer.
