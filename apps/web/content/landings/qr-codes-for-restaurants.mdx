Most restaurants need four QR codes: the menu, the guest Wi-Fi, a review link and, if you take orders online, an ordering or booking page. The menu is the one to make dynamic, so you can change it without reprinting. All four are free on QRfinity, and none of them ever expires.

## Which QR codes does a restaurant need?

| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Menu](/qr-code-generator/menu)                   | Dynamic, so you can update it             | Table tents, stickers, the window                          |

Your menu lives where it already does: a page on your website, your ordering system or a PDF on Google Drive or Dropbox. QRfinity makes the code that points to it.

## How do I make a menu QR code I never have to reprint?

1. Choose **Dynamic** in the generator on this page and paste your menu’s link.
2. Add your logo and colours if you like. The scan check warns you before you download anything that might not scan.
3. Save it to your free account and download it.

When the menu changes, either update it at the same link or point the code at the new one from your dashboard. The printed code stays the same, and changes are live within about a minute.

## Where should the codes go, and how big?

- **Table tents and stickers:** 3 to 4 cm wide is right for someone reading from their seat.
- **Windows and walls:** about one tenth of the distance people stand from it, so 30 cm for a sign read from 3 metres.
- **Receipts and bill folders:** at least 2 cm wide, printed dark on light paper. Thermal printers fade, so test an old receipt.
- **Leave a blank margin** around every code. Text or a table edge right against it is a common reason codes fail.

The [print size guide](/guides/qr-code-size-for-print) has the full rule.

## How many people scan the menu?

Every dynamic code has scan analytics: total and unique scans, the hours people scan, devices and countries, with up to 2 years of history. Give the patio its own code and you can see which area scans most, or when the lunch rush really starts. We don’t store anyone’s IP address.
