## Why can’t a static QR code expire?

A static QR code stores your content in its pattern. A web address, a Wi-Fi password or a contact card is written directly into the black and white squares, and a phone reads it straight from there. No server sits in between, so there’s nothing for anyone to switch off.

That’s true of static codes from any generator, not just ours. On QRfinity they’re made entirely in your browser, with no sign-up, in unlimited numbers, and they never touch our servers. They’ll keep working as long as the print is readable and the thing they point to exists.

## Why do some dynamic QR codes expire?

A dynamic code stores a short link that belongs to the company that made it. Every scan goes through that company’s server, which forwards the visitor to your destination. That’s what lets you edit the destination and count scans. It also means the company decides whether your code keeps working.

## What should “no expiration” actually mean?

Plenty of pages say “free QR codes” or even “no expiration” and mean only static codes, or only until a trial ends. Here’s what we think the words have to mean, and what we commit to:

- **No trial.** Nothing changes after 7, 14 or 30 days.
- **No scan cap.** A code doesn’t stop after 100, 500 or 10,000 scans.
- **No deactivation.** We never switch off a free code because of its age, its plan or how long since you last logged in.
- **Not paused for not paying.** Your codes don’t depend on a subscription, now or later.

That covers both static and dynamic codes, and it’s in our [terms](/terms), not just on this page.

## What happens if QRfinity ever shuts down?

It’s a fair question to ask of any service. Here are the facts.

**Static codes keep working no matter what.** They don’t use our servers, so nothing we do, including closing, affects them. If you want a code with zero dependency on any company, make it static.

**Dynamic codes rely on our redirect service** on qrfinity.link, the same way any dynamic code relies on the company that issued it. You can export all your codes and destinations at any time from your account page, as CSV or JSON, including each code’s short link and full destination history. That means you always hold your own record of what every printed code points to.

We’d rather be plain about this than promise something we can’t show you. If dependence on any company is a deal-breaker, static is the right choice, and our [static vs dynamic guide](/guides/static-vs-dynamic-qr-codes) helps you decide.
