QR codes make it easy for people to give, read the bulletin, follow the sermon notes, sign up for events and ask for prayer, from their own phone, in the pew or at home. One dynamic code on the noticeboard or pew card can open this week’s bulletin every week. QRfinity’s codes are free and never expire.

## Which codes a church needs

| Use | Code | What it opens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Giving | [Payment link](/qr-code-generator/payment-link) or [PayPal](/qr-code-generator/paypal) | Your online giving page |
| Bulletin, sermon notes | Dynamic link | This week’s bulletin or notes |
| Events | [Event](/qr-code-generator/event) | Adds the date and place to the calendar |
| Prayer requests, sign-ups | [Google Forms](/qr-code-generator/google-forms) | The form |
| Visitors | [Contact card](/qr-code-generator/vcard) | The office’s phone and email |
| Hall, café | [Wi-Fi](/qr-code-generator/wifi) | The guest network |

## One code, every Sunday

Print a dynamic code once, on the pew cards, the noticeboard and the back of the bulletin, and point it at this week’s bulletin or notes. Each week, update the destination from your dashboard, or let a [date limit](/guides/change-qr-code-after-date-or-scans) switch it automatically.

## Giving codes people can trust

Giving codes in public places are a target for fake stickers. Print them into your signs and cards, put the church’s name and the giving address beside them, and check them before each service. Our [guide to tampering](/guides/protect-qr-codes-from-tampering) has more.

## Where to put codes

- **Pew cards and bulletins:** 2.5 to 3 cm.
- **Noticeboards and entrance signs:** 10 to 15 cm.
- **Screens:** about one tenth of the distance to the back row, left up long enough to scan.
