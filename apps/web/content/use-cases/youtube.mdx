## What a YouTube QR code does

A YouTube QR code takes someone from a printed page to a video or channel in one scan. That’s handy when a picture isn’t enough: assembling furniture, setting up a device, cooking a recipe on the back of a pack, or seeing the band before buying a ticket.

Makers print them in manuals and on packaging, teachers put them on worksheets, and creators add them to merch and business cards to grow subscribers.

## How to make a YouTube QR code

1. **Check that YouTube is selected.** This page picks the Social type with “Platform” set to YouTube.
2. **Add your channel or video.** In “Username or profile link”, type your channel handle or paste a full video link, including any timestamp.
3. **Make it yours.** Choose colours, add a logo and a frame with text such as “Watch the video”.
4. **Save or download.** Save it as a dynamic code to your free account if you might swap the video later, or download a static code now.

## Design and print tips

- **Say what they’ll watch.** “Watch the 2-minute setup video” beats “Scan me”. People scan more when they know the payoff and how long it takes.
- **Put it where the question comes up.** In a manual, place the code on the step people get stuck on, not only on the back cover.
- **Size for small print.** On packaging and inserts, keep it at least 2 cm wide, and give it a margin of 4 modules or more.
- **Avoid curved or creased spots.** On a bottle or a folded box edge, the camera sees a distorted code. Use a flat panel.
- **Keep the video public or unlisted.** A private video shows an error to everyone who scans.

## Channel, video or playlist?

Match the link to what the reader needs at that moment:

- **A single video** for one task, such as fitting a filter or pairing headphones. Add a timestamp if the useful part starts a minute in.
- **A playlist** for a product with several steps or models. Share the playlist from YouTube and paste its full link; you can add videos to it later without changing the code.
- **Your channel** for business cards, shop windows and event banners, where the goal is subscribers rather than one view.

If you’re not sure which to choose, a dynamic code lets you start with one and switch later.
