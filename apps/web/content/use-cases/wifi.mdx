## What a Wi-Fi QR code does

A Wi-Fi QR code stores your network name, password and security type in the standard format phones understand. When someone points their camera at it, the phone offers to join the network. They don’t type anything, so nobody gets a letter wrong and you don’t have to spell out “capital P, zero, not O” at the counter.

It suits cafés, restaurants, rental homes, offices, clinics and events: anywhere guests ask for the Wi-Fi more than once a day.

## How to make a Wi-Fi QR code

1. **Enter your network name.** Type the name (SSID) exactly as it appears in your phone’s Wi-Fi list. Capitals and spaces matter.
2. **Choose the security type and enter the password.** Most routers use WPA, WPA2 or WPA3; pick “No password” for an open network.
3. **Check the scan result.** The preview updates as you type, and we decode it like a phone camera would. Test it with your own phone too: it should offer to join your network.
4. **Make it yours, then download.** Add a frame with the text “Wi-Fi” so people know what the code is for, then download a PNG for screens or a PDF or SVG for printing.

## Design and print tips

- **Say what it is.** A frame that reads “Wi-Fi” or “Scan to join our Wi-Fi” gets far more scans than a bare code. Add the network name in small text underneath for people whose camera won’t cooperate.
- **Size it for the distance.** On a table tent or counter card, 3 to 4 cm wide is plenty. On a wall poster read from 2 metres away, aim for at least 20 cm. A rough rule: width of about one tenth of the scanning distance.
- **Keep the contrast high.** Dark code on a light background. Avoid printing on glossy laminate under bright lights; matte paper scans more reliably.
- **Print one per table or room.** People scan from their seat, not across the room.
- **Use a guest network.** Most routers let you create one in a few minutes. It keeps visitors away from your printers, cameras and computers.
