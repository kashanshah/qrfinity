## What wedding QR codes do

QR codes save your guests from typing long links from a card: one scan to RSVP, find the venue, see the schedule or add their photos. On the day, a code on each table can collect every guest’s photos in one shared album.

## How to make a wedding QR code

1. **Choose what the code opens first.** Usually the RSVP form or your wedding website.
2. **Paste the link into Website address.** This page starts with a dynamic code, so you can change it later.
3. **Match your stationery.** Pick colours from your invitations, a dot pattern, and a frame like “RSVP here”.
4. **Save it and test it.** Save the code to your free account, scan a printed test with two phones, then print.
5. **Change it as the day goes.** After the RSVP deadline, point it at the schedule; after the wedding, at the photo album.

## Design and print tips

- **Contrast beats colour.** Blush, sage and gold are lovely for the frame; keep the code itself dark enough to stand out from the card.
- **Size for the card.** 2.5 cm on invitations and place cards, 5 cm on table cards, 20 cm on a welcome sign.
- **Test on your actual paper.** Textured, shimmer and vellum papers can make codes harder to read.
- **Add a short line of text.** “Scan to RSVP by 1 June” tells older guests exactly what to do.
