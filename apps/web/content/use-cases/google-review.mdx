## What a Google review QR code does

A Google review QR code opens your business’s review form on Google. The customer scans, chooses a star rating and writes a few words, without searching for your business or scrolling through the map listing. Removing those steps is what turns “I’ll leave you a review later” into a review today.

Restaurants, salons, dentists, mechanics, hotels, gyms and shops put them on counters, receipts, invoices, takeaway bags and thank-you cards: anywhere the customer is at the end of a good visit.

## How to get your review link

1. Sign in to the Google account that manages your business, then search for your business name on Google or open it in Google Maps.
2. In your Business Profile, choose “Ask for reviews” or “Get more reviews”. The wording varies a little between Search and Maps.
3. Copy the short link Google shows you. Open it on your phone once to check that it lands on your review form.

## How to make a Google review QR code

1. **Paste your review link.** Put the link from Google into “Website address”.
2. **Choose Dynamic.** Under “Type of code”, pick Dynamic so you can change the destination later and count scans.
3. **Add a frame.** Choose a frame style and use the suggested text “Review us”, or write your own, such as “Enjoyed your visit? Review us on Google”.
4. **Save your dynamic code.** Sign in with an email link or Google to save it to your free account, then download a PNG or JPG, or SVG, PDF or EPS with a plan.

<Callout tone="warning">
Google’s review policies prohibit offering incentives for reviews and asking only happy customers to leave one. Wording like “10% off for a 5-star review” or showing the code only after a satisfaction check can get reviews removed. Ask everyone, plainly.
</Callout>

## Design and print tips

- **Ask at the right moment.** Place the code where people are when the service is done: the till, the receipt, the bill folder or the bag.
- **Use neutral wording.** “Tell us how we did” or “Review us on Google” works and stays within Google’s rules. Don’t mention star ratings.
- **Size it for the counter.** A counter stand or table card needs a code about 3 to 5 cm wide. On a receipt, 2 to 2.5 cm is enough if the printer is sharp.
- **Check thermal receipt prints.** Receipt printers can blur fine detail. Print a test receipt and scan it before relying on it.
- **Add your logo.** A recognisable logo reassures people that the code belongs to you and isn’t a sticker someone added.

## Static or dynamic?

<Recommendation pick="dynamic">
Use a dynamic code. If Google changes your review link, you move to another review site, or you open a second location, you change the destination instead of reprinting every counter card. You also see how many people scan it.
</Recommendation>
