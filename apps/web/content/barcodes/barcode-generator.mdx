## Barcode or QR code?

A barcode holds a short number or code, read by shop scanners, warehouse guns and stock systems. A QR code holds much more, such as a web address, Wi-Fi details or a contact card, and every phone camera reads it. If people will scan it with their phone, use a [QR code](/qr-code-generator). If a till, a warehouse or a stock system will scan it, use a barcode.

## How big should a barcode be?

Retail barcodes have a standard size: a UPC-A or EAN-13 at 100% is about 37 mm wide including its blank margins, and you can print it from 80% to 200% of that. Don’t squash the height to fit a tight space; cut the bars shorter only if your printer or retailer allows it.

For Code 128 and other label barcodes, keep the narrowest bar at least 0.19 mm wide, and wider if people will scan from a distance. The generator shows the bar width as you change the size, and warns you when it gets too thin.

<Callout>
  Leave the blank margins on both sides. Scanners need that quiet space to find where the barcode
  starts and ends, and trimming it is the most common reason a printed barcode won’t scan.
</Callout>

## Which file should I download?

- **PNG:** for most printing, documents and labels. Every bar is a whole number of pixels, so edges stay sharp and scanners read the widths exactly.
- **JPG:** where PNG isn’t accepted.
- **SVG, PDF, EPS:** vector files for packaging and print shops, sharp at any size. They come with a paid plan, from $5 a month.
